# PotassiumCheck
### A Digital Prototype for Screening Potassium-Containing Food Additives

🌐 **Live Website:** [https://aqsaakanwal.github.io/PotassiumCheck/](https://aqsaakanwal.github.io/PotassiumCheck/)

## About the Project

PotassiumCheck is an educational and research-oriented digital prototype designed to screen ingredient labels of processed foods for selected potassium-containing food additives.

The project explores how food composition information and digital screening tools could potentially support nutrition assessment in individuals with Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), particularly in the context of dietary potassium management.

## Key Features

- **Ingredient Analyzer:** Screens ingredient lists for selected potassium-containing additives.
- **Additive Database:** Identifies additives using their names, E-numbers, and recognized aliases.
- **Dashboard:** Displays product screening records and additive detection summaries.
- **Research Database:** Stores product information, screening results, and research notes.
- **Laboratory Data Section:** Provides fields to record verified potassium measurements when laboratory results become available.
- **Export Options:** Supports CSV export and print functionality.

## Research Focus

The prototype currently focuses on selected potassium-containing additives, including:

- Potassium Chloride (E508)
- Potassium Sorbate (E202)
- Potassium Phosphates (E340 series)
- Potassium Citrates (E332 series)
- Other selected potassium-containing additives.

## Methodology

The prototype uses ingredient-label screening to identify selected potassium-containing additives in processed food products. It provides a preliminary digital approach to organizing food composition information for further research.

## Scientific Limitations

PotassiumCheck is an initial screening prototype and has not been clinically or scientifically validated.

- Ingredient-label screening does not determine the actual quantity of potassium in a product.
- Ingredient order does not indicate the exact concentration of an additive.
- It does not measure total potassium content or estimate individual dietary potassium intake.
- It does not assess potassium bioavailability or clinical risk.
- Laboratory analysis is required to determine actual potassium concentrations.

## Future Development

Potential future improvements include:

- Laboratory-based potassium quantification.
- Integration of verified food composition data.
- Expansion of the additive database using authoritative references.
- Further development in collaboration with nutrition assessment research.
