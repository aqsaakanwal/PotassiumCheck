Research Methodology

1. Research Objective

To develop an educational digital prototype for screening selected potassium-containing additives in processed food ingredient labels and organizing product-level information for future CKD nutrition assessment research.

2. Research Design

A descriptive ingredient-label screening approach is used. Product labels are reviewed for selected additive names and associated E-numbers.

3. Data Collection

Product name, brand, category, serving size and ingredient-list information are recorded from packaged food labels. Laboratory measurements and references can be added separately.

4. Screening Procedure

  1. Select a packaged food product.
  2. Record its ingredient list as printed on the package.
  3. Enter the information into PotassiumCheck.
  4. Compare ingredient names and selected E-numbers against the prototype database.
  5. Save identified additives and product information.
  6. Record laboratory measurements only when actual data are available.
  7. Export records for further research analysis.

5. Data Analysis

Product counts, additive frequencies and laboratory measurements can be summarized separately. Laboratory values must be interpreted according to the analytical method and sample basis.

6. Future Experimental Validation

Selected products could undergo laboratory potassium quantification using an appropriate validated analytical method, such as flame photometry or ICP-OES, depending on available facilities, sample preparation and method validation.

7. Research Limitations

  • Ingredient labels generally do not state the quantity of each additive.
  • Ingredient order is not a quantitative measurement.
  • The prototype database covers selected additives, not every possible potassium source.
  • Spelling and label transcription can affect detection.
  • Laboratory measurements are not automatically available.
  • The prototype does not estimate individual potassium intake or clinical risk.

About PotassiumCheck

PotassiumCheck is an educational research prototype connecting food ingredient information, food composition research and nutrition assessment in individuals with chronic kidney disease.


Food Composition

Identifies selected potassium-containing additives in ingredient labels.


Laboratory Research

Provides fields for recording measured potassium concentrations and analytical methods.


Scientific Documentation

Allows researchers to organize references, laboratory notes and product-level data.

PotassiumCheck is not a validated clinical instrument and does not provide individualized dietary advice. Clinical decisions for CKD should be made with qualified healthcare professionals.
